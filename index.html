Field guide

The quiet mechanics of speed.

Fast typists do not try harder. They remove friction: eyes on the screen, fingers on the anchors, and just enough tension to press a key.

01
The one rule

Do not look down.

Looking at your hands feels safe, but it trains your eyes to do work your fingers should own. Glance away, reset on the F and J bumps, and let the map form in your hands. It will feel clumsy for a day or two. Then it will feel automatic.

“Accuracy is not the opposite of speed. It is the door.”
Posture

Settle in, then forget it.

  • 01

    Feet flat, hips back, screen near eye level.

  • 02

    Elbows close to 90°, wrists floating, fingers curved.

  • 03

    Index fingers resting on F and J.

  • 04

    Strike with the fingertip, then release the tension.

Before you start

Warm the hands, not the ego.

Sixty seconds of easy movement makes the first line feel less like a cold start.

01

Unclench. Shake out the hands, roll the shoulders back, and drop the jaw.

02

Wake the fingers. Tap each fingertip to the thumb, slowly, one hand at a time.

03

First line slow. Type one sentence at half speed and find the home-row bumps.

The map

One finger per zone.

Each color is a finger zone. Your index fingers cover two columns each; your thumbs own the space bar.

Code mode

When the text is code.

Both hands · no mouse
01Shift with the opposite pinky.Right-hand symbol, left Shift. Left-hand symbol, right Shift.
02Land the pairs.Brackets, braces and quotes come in twos. Type the pair as one thought.
03Own the punctuation.Semicolons, colons and underscores live on the right pinky and ring fingers.
04Keep a code rhythm.Accuracy on symbols matters more than raw speed. Read the line before you type it.

A 20-minute session

Small, steady, repeatable.

5 min

Reset. Slow drills on the lesson's focus keys. Perfection is the only goal.

10 min

Build. Type words and sentences at a pace where you make almost no errors.

5 min

Release. One fast pass. Let accuracy carry the speed instead of forcing it.
